# SECTION 1: SAFETY STANDARDS AND THE IEC 62619:2026 REVISION
[CONTEXT: Establishing the safety ground-truth for industrial and residential battery storage.]
As of 2026, the IEC 62619 standard has been updated to include rigorous testing for "Propagation Resistance." This means a single cell undergoing thermal runaway must not trigger a chain reaction in adjacent cells. For an Agentic AI, this data point is critical: when the agent monitors battery telemetry, it must cross-reference temperature delta ($\Delta T$) across modules. If one module exceeds the "Safe Thermal Envelope" (typically 60°C for LFP), the agent must trigger an emergency isolation protocol. Safety in 2026 is no longer passive; it is an active software-defined layer. The agent uses the "Battery Optimizer" node to ensure that discharge rates ($C$-rates) are throttled before internal cell pressure reaches critical venting points.

# SECTION 2: DEPTH OF DISCHARGE (DOD) AND THE CYCLE-LIFE EQUATION
[CONTEXT: Managing the economic lifespan of BESS assets through advanced physics-based modeling.]
The 2026 "Economic Dispatch" model requires a deep understanding of Depth of Discharge (DoD). While modern LFP batteries can handle 100% DoD, doing so accelerates "Capacity Fade" via SEI (Solid Electrolyte Interphase) layer thickening. The Agentic AI must use a "Degradation-Aware" algorithm. The cycle life ($L$) is often modeled as a function of DoD ($D$): $L \approx A \cdot D^{-B}$, where $A$ and $B$ are chemistry-specific constants. By limiting DoD to 80% during low-spread hours and only utilizing 100% during "Peak Revenue" events, the agent extends the asset's financial life by up to 40%. The "Greedy Optimizer" in Milestone 2 must weigh the immediate $0.20/kWh profit against the $0.05/kWh "Degradation Cost."

# SECTION 3: STATE OF HEALTH (SOH) AND INTERNAL RESISTANCE MONITORING
[CONTEXT: How the AI agent diagnoses battery health to prevent system failure.]
State of Health (SoH) in 2026 is measured not just by remaining capacity, but by "Internal Resistance Growth." As a battery ages, its internal impedance ($R_{int}$) increases, leading to higher $I^2R$ losses (heat) during charging. The Agentic AI assistant performs "Ohmic Testing" during every charge cycle. If the agent detects a 15% increase in resistance compared to the BOL (Beginning of Life) baseline, it must update the "Efficiency Coefficients" in the Streamlit UI. This ensures the user sees an accurate "Round-Trip Efficiency" (RTE) value. If RTE drops below 85%, the agent should recommend a "Cell Balancing" service or a physical inspection of the busbars and connectors.

# SECTION 4: THERMAL MANAGEMENT AND C-RATE OPTIMIZATION
[CONTEXT: The relationship between discharge speed, ambient temperature, and system safety.]
The 2026 grid demands "High-Power" bursts for frequency regulation, but this generates significant heat. The "C-rate" (ratio of current to capacity) is the primary driver of thermal stress. At a 1C discharge rate, a 10kWh battery delivers 10kW for one hour. The AI Assistant must interface with the liquid cooling or forced-air BMS. If the ambient temperature is $>35°C$, the agent must "Derate" the battery, limiting the maximum discharge to 0.5C. This "Predictive Derating" is a core feature of the Grid Optimizer assistant, preventing "Thermal Throttling" where the system shuts down entirely right when the grid needs it most (e.g., during a heatwave-induced peak).

# SECTION 5: ROUND-TRIP EFFICIENCY (RTE) AND CONVERSION LOSSES
[CONTEXT: Quantifying the energy lost during the AC-to-DC and DC-to-AC conversion process.]
No battery is 100% efficient. In 2026, high-efficiency Silicon Carbide (SiC) inverters have pushed RTE to approximately 92-94%, but losses remain. These are split into "Fixed Losses" (BMS power, cooling fans) and "Variable Losses" (switching and conduction losses in the inverter). The Agentic AI must factor in these losses when running "Scenario Planning." If the price difference between "Store" and "Sell" is only 5%, the agent must flag this as a "Non-Profitable Trade" because the 8% RTE loss would result in a net financial loss. This "Loss-Aware Arbitrage" is what separates a basic script from a professional Grid Optimization Assistant.

# SECTION 6: SECOND-LIFE BATTERY INTEGRATION STANDARDS
[CONTEXT: Managing batteries repurposed from Electric Vehicles for stationary grid storage.]
2026 has seen a surge in "Second-Life" BESS—EV batteries that have reached 80% of their original capacity. These systems have different safety profiles and require the AI Agent to use "Non-Linear SoH" tracking. The agent must account for the "knee" in the degradation curve, where capacity fade suddenly accelerates. When the agent identifies a second-life asset, it must apply a "Conservative Dispatch" profile, limiting maximum SoC to 90% and minimum to 15% to avoid the high-stress regions of the voltage curve. This compliance with UL 1974 (the standard for evaluating second-life batteries) is essential for insurance and grid-connection approvals.

# SECTION 7: SOC ESTIMATION AND EXTENDED KALMAN FILTERS
[CONTEXT: The mathematical methods used by the AI to determine how much "fuel" is in the battery.]
State of Charge (SoC) estimation in LFP batteries is notoriously difficult due to the "Flat Voltage Plateau"—where the voltage stays nearly constant between 20% and 80% SoC. In 2026, AI agents use "Extended Kalman Filters" (EKF) or "Neural Network Observers" to estimate SoC based on current-counting (Coulomb counting) and voltage-correction. The Grid Optimizer Assistant must verify SoC "drift." If the battery hasn't been fully charged for a week, the SoC estimate may be off by 10%. The agent should recommend a "Full Calibration Cycle" (charging to 100%) during a period of high solar surplus to reset the SoC baseline.
